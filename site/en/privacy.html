Wake Wake Up

Privacy policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026.

This is a translation of the Portuguese policy. Both say the same thing.

In short

Wake Wake Up has no sign-up, no ads and no tracking tools. Your alarms and your history are stored on your phone. The app only uses the internet for the radio feature: when you open the radio screen to pick a station and when an alarm plays a radio station.

Who is responsible

Wake Wake Up is made by CyberHat. For anything about privacy, write to cyberhat.tech@gmail.com.

What is stored on your phone

To work, the app stores in your phone's internal storage:

  • Your alarms: time, name, days of the week, whether they are on, the mission type, the difficulty and number of problems, the chosen sound (with the station address, if it is a radio station), whether the volume increases gradually and the day an alarm will skip, when you ask it to skip a day.
  • Your wake-up history: the day and time of the alarm, the time you completed the mission, how many times you used Snooze and how long it took.
  • Simple app preferences, such as a note that you have already seen the permissions screen.

The app shows the last 7 days, but the history stays on the phone until you clear the app's data or uninstall it. None of this is sent to us or to anyone else. We have no access to it.

Notice before an alarm

If an alarm set between 03:00 and 11:59 is due within 1 hour and you are already using your phone (screen on and unlocked), the app may show a silent notification asking whether you want to turn that alarm off just this once. To confirm, you have to open the app and solve a quick sum. Without that confirmation, the alarm stays set.

This check happens only on the phone. The app only checks whether the screen is on and unlocked: it does not see which apps you use or what is on the screen, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

If you don't want this notice, turn off the Before an alarm notification category in Wake Wake Up's notification settings on Android. On a phone set to Portuguese, it is called Aviso antes do alarme.

When the app uses the internet

Only for the radio feature. If you never open the radio screen or choose a radio station as a sound, the app makes no connection at all.

  • Station search. When you open the radio screen, the app asks Radio Browser (radio-browser.info), a free public directory of radio stations, for the most listened stations in the country set on your phone. For that it sends only the country code, such as BR. If you type a search, the text you type is sent instead. As with any connection, that service receives your IP address.
  • Playing the station. For the preview and when the alarm rings, the app connects directly to the chosen station's server to receive the audio. The station receives your IP address, as it would if you listened in a browser.

These are third-party services with their own privacy policies. We get no copy of what you search for or what you listen to.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask for sign-up, email, phone number or any identification.
  • It shows no ads and uses no analytics or tracking tools.
  • It does not access contacts, location, camera or microphone.
  • It does not see which other apps you use.
  • It does not sell or share data.

When you choose music from your phone as the alarm sound, the app only plays the file you selected. It does not copy or send that file.

Android backup

If Android backup is turned on on your phone, the system itself may copy your alarms and your history to the backup in your Google account, as it does for other apps. That way they can come back if you switch phones or reinstall the app. The app preferences are left out of that backup. The backup is made by Android, kept in your Google account and controlled in your phone's settings. We have no access to it.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Notifications: show the ringing alarm, an active Snooze and the notice before an alarm.
  • Exact alarms: ring on the minute.
  • Full screen while locked: open the alarm and the mission over the lock screen.
  • Run at startup: set the alarms again after the phone restarts.
  • Keep the phone awake and foreground service: keep the alarm ringing until the mission is done.
  • Vibration: vibrate while the alarm rings.
  • Internet: search for and play radio stations, as described above.

The app does not ask to be exempted from battery optimization. If your phone holds alarms back to save battery, the app can open Android's battery settings so you can adjust them yourself.

App testing

If you send an email asking for test access, we use your address only to reply and to add you to the test. For that, it may be registered as a tester on Google Play. You can ask to leave the test at any time by replying to the same email.

This website

This website uses no cookies or analytics and loads nothing from other websites. If you change the language with the PT / EN switch, your browser keeps that choice on this device, only so the site opens in the same language next time. It is not sent to us. Like any website, it runs on a hosting server (Hostinger), which may log technical access data such as IP address, browser and time.

How to delete your data

Since everything stays on your phone, you can delete it whenever you like: uninstall the app, or open Android's settings, go to Apps, Wake Wake Up, Storage and tap Clear data. If Android backup is on, the copy in your Google account is controlled in the backup settings of your phone and your Google account.

Your rights

Brazil's General Data Protection Law (Lei 13.709/2018, LGPD) gives you, among others, the right to know whether your data is processed, to access it, correct it and ask for it to be deleted. To use these rights or ask questions, write to cyberhat.tech@gmail.com.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts handling data differently, this page will be updated, with the new date at the top.
